## Input

### Species

Sets the codon usage table the optimizer adapts to. Pick the organism
you'll express the sequence in — _H. sapiens_ and _M. musculus_ have
different preferred-synonym frequencies, and the wrong choice will pull
codon optimization toward the wrong tRNA pool.

### Split point

The position where REJ Studio divides your CDS into 5′ and 3′
fragments. The slider defaults to the
[WGGW motif](/docs/reference/glossary#wggw-motif) closest to the
midpoint — a balanced split that places a WGGW substrate at the joint.
Drag it to pick a different motif if you have a reason to. Note:
"split point" refers to the REJ cleavage position and is distinct from
a spliceosomal splice junction.

## Soft objectives

Soft objectives are things the solver _tries_ to optimize. Each has a
weight controlling how much it pulls on the final sequence relative to
the others. See [weights](#parameter-weights) below.

### Remove cryptic splice sites [#remove-cryptic-splice-sites]

Penalizes cryptic 5′ donor and 3′ acceptor motifs in each fragment and
drives the solver toward synonymous rewrites that break them.
Recommended for any design that will be transcribed in a splicing-
competent cell — unintended splicing can silently excise stretches of
the CDS and produce truncated or frame-shifted proteins. Because this
is a soft objective (`AvoidPattern` in DNAChisel), strong motifs may
persist when they conflict with higher-priority constraints.

### Minimize CpG sites [#minimize-cpgs]

Reduces the number of
[CpG dinucleotides](/docs/reference/glossary#cpg-dinucleotide).
Unmethylated CpGs in delivered nucleic acids are a major driver of
TLR9-mediated innate immune sensing; for constructs that integrate,
CpG-rich promoters are also preferential methylation substrates, which
can silence transcription. Lowering CpG count is standard practice for
therapeutic RNA and many synthetic constructs.

### Reduce k-mer complexity [#kmer-complexity]

Pushes the solver toward unique 10-base _k_-mers across each fragment
(DNAChisel's `UniquifyAllKmers`).
[Repetitive sequences](/docs/reference/glossary#k-mer-complexity)
synthesize less reliably and are more prone to homologous
recombination during assembly; enforcing uniqueness at this window
length typically reduces both failure modes.

## Hard constraints

Hard constraints must be satisfied. If the solver can't meet one, the
job reports it as violated.

### Enforce 35–60% GC content [#gc-content]

Keeps GC content inside the 35–60% window typical of stable mammalian
CDSs, enforced over a sliding window (DNAChisel's `EnforceGCContent`,
`mini=0.35`, `maxi=0.60`). The GC sparkline visualizes how content
varies across the sequence:

If the solver can't hit the window given your other constraints,
you'll see it called out in the report. Leave this on unless you have
a specific reason to run without a GC constraint.

## Stimulatory introns

Optional. REJ Studio marks a `[STIMINTRON]` placeholder in either
fragment at a WGGW-compatible position ~150 bp from the split. The
tool itself does not embed an intron sequence — you substitute your
chosen intron at the placeholder during synthesis. Intron-mediated
enhancement (coupling to splicing, export, and translation) typically
raises transcript and protein levels, but the magnitude is
construct- and context-dependent.

### 5′ stimulatory intron [#stim-5]

Marks a placeholder upstream of the split, in the 5′ fragment. Useful
when the 5′ half is the limiting piece for expression.

### 3′ stimulatory intron [#stim-3]

Marks a placeholder downstream of the split, in the 3′ fragment.
Useful when the 3′ half is the limiting piece.

Enable one, both, or neither based on downstream context. Placeholders
stack cleanly with the rest of the optimization.

## Parameter weights [#parameter-weights]

Each soft objective has a weight that controls how much it pulls on
the final sequence relative to the others. You only need to touch
these when the defaults give you a result that's good at one thing
and weak at another.

| Weight | What it controls |
|---|---|
| **Codon optimization** | How hard the solver pushes toward the species-preferred codon table. |
| **Cryptic splice site** | How hard it pushes to eliminate splice-like motifs. |
| **CpG minimization** | How hard it pushes CpG count down. |
| **K-mer complexity** | How hard it pushes to break up repeated k-mers. |

Hard constraints (GC content) always take priority over any weight.
