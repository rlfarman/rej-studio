## Biology

### CDS (coding sequence)

The stretch of DNA that translates into protein — from the `ATG` start
codon to the stop codon. REJ Studio's optimizer operates on CDS, not
UTRs, introns, or full genomic sequences.

### Isoform (transcript)

A distinct mRNA produced from a gene. A single gene typically has
multiple isoforms that differ in exon usage, start site, or stop site,
producing related but non-identical proteins. REJ Studio's gene pages
list every annotated isoform.

### Codon

A group of three bases that encodes one amino acid. Most amino acids
have multiple synonymous codons — different DNA, same protein — which is
what lets the optimizer rewrite a sequence without changing what it
codes for.

### CpG dinucleotide

A cytosine followed by a guanine on the same strand. CpGs are substrates
for DNA methylation and can trigger innate immune sensing via TLR9. For
therapeutic constructs you typically want fewer of them.

### Cryptic splice site

A sequence motif that resembles a real 5' or 3' splice site enough that
the spliceosome may accidentally use it. If your construct is
transcribed in a cell with active splicing, a cryptic site can delete
part of your coding sequence silently. Removing them keeps the intended
CDS intact.

### WGGW motif

A four-base motif where `W = A or T`, i.e. `[AT]GG[AT]` — `AGGA`,
`AGGT`, `TGGA`, or `TGGT`. These are the recognition substrates for RNA
End-Joining, and REJ Studio uses them to choose where to split a coding
sequence into 5' and 3' fragments.

### Splice junction

The position in a CDS where REJ Studio splits the sequence into two
fragments. The splitter prefers WGGW motifs closest to the midpoint so
the two halves are roughly balanced and both carry a valid REJ substrate
at the joint.

### Stimulatory intron

A short, optimized intron inserted into a fragment to increase
expression. REJ Studio can optionally add a 5' or 3' stimulatory intron
to either fragment; the protein is unchanged but the transcript is
processed more efficiently in many mammalian contexts.

## Algorithm

### DNAChisel

The open-source codon-optimization library REJ Studio's backend uses.
It treats optimization as a constraint-satisfaction problem: you declare
hard constraints (must be satisfied) and soft objectives (should be
minimized), and the solver searches for a coding-equivalent sequence
that meets them.

### Hard constraint vs. soft objective

A **hard constraint** must be satisfied — if the solver can't meet it,
the job fails or the constraint is reported as violated. GC content in
REJ Studio is a hard constraint. A **soft objective** is something the
solver tries to minimize (lower is better) alongside everything else;
codon usage and k-mer complexity are soft objectives, with per-objective
weights that control how much each one pulls on the final sequence.

### K-mer complexity

A measure of how repetitive a sequence is at a given window length. A
sequence with many repeated 10-mers is low-complexity and harder to
synthesize, assemble, and sequence cleanly. REJ Studio's optimizer
penalizes repeated k-mers so the result is easier to manufacture.

### Codon adaptation

Rewriting each codon to favor the most-used synonym for a target
organism. Human and mouse have different usage tables, so picking the
right species matters if expression efficiency is the goal.

## Output

### Optimization report

The per-objective breakdown returned after a job finishes: which
constraints were satisfied, which objectives were minimized, and how
much each one changed from the input. See
[Reading your results](/docs/guides/reading-results).

### Diagnostic badges

Quick-read indicators shown above the optimized sequence — GC%, CpG
count, max k-mer repeat, length. Use them for a glance-level sanity
check; use the full report for the details.
