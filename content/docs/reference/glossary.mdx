## Biology

### CDS (coding sequence)

The stretch of DNA that translates into protein — from the `ATG` start
codon to the stop codon. REJ Studio's optimizer operates on CDS, not
UTRs, introns, or full genomic sequences.

### Isoform (transcript)

A distinct transcript variant produced from a gene. A single gene
typically has multiple isoforms that differ in exon composition,
transcription start site, or polyadenylation site. They usually encode
related — but not always identical — proteins; some isoforms differ
only in untranslated regions and produce the same protein. REJ Studio's
gene pages list every annotated isoform.

### Codon

A group of three bases that encodes one amino acid. Most amino acids
have multiple synonymous codons — different DNA, same protein — which is
what lets the optimizer rewrite a sequence without changing what it
codes for.

### CpG dinucleotide

A cytosine immediately 5′ of a guanine on the same strand
(5′-C-p-G-3′). Unmethylated CpGs in delivered nucleic acids are
recognized by TLR9 and drive innate immune responses; for constructs
that integrate into genomic DNA, CpG-rich regions are also preferential
substrates for DNA methylation, which can silence transcription. For
therapeutic constructs you typically want fewer of them.

### Cryptic splice site

A sequence motif that resembles a bona fide 5′ donor or 3′ acceptor
splice site closely enough that the spliceosome may use it instead of
(or in addition to) the intended site. If your construct is transcribed
in a cell with active splicing machinery, a cryptic site can cause
silent excision of a portion of the CDS, producing a truncated or
frame-shifted protein. Avoiding these motifs keeps the intended reading
frame intact.

### WGGW motif

A four-base motif where `W = A or T`, i.e. `[AT]GG[AT]` — `AGGA`,
`AGGT`, `TGGA`, or `TGGT`. These are the recognition substrates for RNA
End-Joining, and REJ Studio uses them to choose where to split a coding
sequence into 5' and 3' fragments.

### Split point

The position in a CDS where REJ Studio divides the sequence into 5′ and
3′ fragments. The splitter prefers WGGW motifs closest to the midpoint
so the two halves are roughly balanced and both carry a WGGW substrate
at the joint. "Split point" is distinct from a spliceosomal splice
junction (an exon–intron boundary); it refers only to where the
construct is cleaved for REJ delivery. A balanced split looks like

### Stimulatory intron

A short intron placed in a transcript to raise steady-state mRNA and
protein levels via intron-mediated enhancement (coupling to splicing,
export, and translation). REJ Studio does not embed an intron sequence
itself — when this option is enabled it marks a `[STIMINTRON]`
placeholder in the output FASTA at a WGGW-compatible position roughly
150 bp from the split. You substitute your chosen intron sequence at
that marker during synthesis. The protein is unchanged; enhancement is
expected but is not guaranteed for every construct or cell context.

## Algorithm

### DNAChisel

The open-source codon-optimization library REJ Studio's backend uses.
It treats optimization as a constraint-satisfaction problem: you declare
hard constraints (must be satisfied) and soft objectives (should be
minimized), and the solver searches for a coding-equivalent sequence
that meets them.

### Hard constraint vs. soft objective

A **hard constraint** must be satisfied — if the solver can't meet it,
the job fails or the constraint is reported as violated. GC content in
REJ Studio is a hard constraint. A **soft objective** is something the
solver tries to minimize (lower is better) alongside everything else;
codon usage and k-mer complexity are soft objectives, with per-objective
weights that control how much each one pulls on the final sequence.

### K-mer complexity

A measure of sequence diversity at a given window length _k_. A
sequence where every _k_-long substring is unique is maximally complex
at that window; one with many repeated _k_-mers is low-complexity.
Repeats of 10+ bases are a common failure mode in commercial DNA
synthesis and can drive homologous recombination during assembly. REJ
Studio's optimizer uses DNAChisel's `UniquifyAllKmers` to push toward
unique 10-mers across each fragment.

### Codon adaptation

Rewriting each codon to favor the synonym preferred by a target
organism's tRNA pool, without changing the encoded protein. Different
species have different usage tables, so picking the right one matters
if translation efficiency is the goal. REJ Studio uses DNAChisel's
`CodonOptimize` spec against _H. sapiens_ or _M. musculus_ frequency
tables.

## Output

### Optimization report

The per-objective breakdown returned after a job finishes: which
constraints were satisfied, which objectives were minimized, and how
much each one changed from the input. See
[Reading your results](/docs/guides/reading-results).

### Diagnostic badges

Quick-read indicators shown above the optimized sequence. Use them
for a glance-level sanity check; use the full report for the details.
