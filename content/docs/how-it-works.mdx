## The short version

REJ Studio is a Next.js frontend over a curated gene database and a Python
optimization backend. You search a gene, pick a transcript, choose a split
point, tune parameters, and submit. The backend splits the sequence at a
[WGGW motif](/docs/glossary#wggw-motif), runs each fragment through
[DNAChisel](https://edinburgh-genome-foundry.github.io/DnaChisel/) with the
constraints you picked, and returns the optimized fragments plus a report.

## The pipeline

<Steps>

<Step>

### Search a gene

Type a symbol, name, Ensembl ID, or disease term. Results are ranked;
matches usually return in under a second. See
[Searching for a gene](/docs/gene-search).

</Step>

<Step>

### Pick an isoform

Every annotated transcript for the gene is listed with length and exon
count. Clicking one opens it in the design tool with its CDS pre-filled.
You can also paste your own CDS — it just needs a start codon, stop
codon, and a length that's a multiple of three.

</Step>

<Step>

### Choose a split point

REJ Studio scans the CDS for WGGW motifs and ranks them by distance to
the midpoint — balanced splits carry real RNA End-Joining substrate at
the joint. You can take the suggested split or override it on the
slider.

</Step>

<Step>

### Tune optimization parameters

Enable the objectives you care about (remove cryptic splice sites,
minimize CpGs, reduce k-mer complexity), pick whether to enforce GC
content, optionally add 5′ or 3′ stimulatory introns, and weight the
objectives if the defaults don't match your priorities. See
[Using the design tool](/docs/design-tool).

</Step>

<Step>

### Submit and wait

The form posts to the Python backend. DNAChisel runs a constraint
solver on each fragment independently, then the results are reassembled
and returned.

</Step>

<Step>

### Review and download

You'll see two optimized fragments, a set of
[diagnostic badges](/docs/glossary#diagnostic-badges), and a full
[optimization report](/docs/results). Download the FASTA before closing
the tab — results are held in session only.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What the optimizer actually does

DNAChisel is a constraint-based codon-optimization library. It doesn't
"invent" sequences — it searches for a coding-equivalent sequence that
satisfies the constraints you picked. Think of it as rewriting the DNA
without changing the amino acids, while steering away from patterns you
don't want (and toward patterns you do).

The [constraints REJ Studio exposes](/docs/design-tool) are the ones
most relevant for RNA End-Joining design: cryptic splice site removal,
CpG minimization, k-mer complexity, and a hard GC content window.

## What it does _not_ do

- It does not predict expression, stability, or immunogenicity.
- It does not validate the biology — if the protein you're encoding is
  toxic, the optimizer will happily optimize it.
- It does not store your submissions long-term. Results are available
  while the job is active; download them before closing the tab.
