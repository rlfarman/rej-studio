title: What is RNA End-Joining?
description: The technique REJ Studio is built around, and why sequence design matters for it.
---

REJ Studio is a design tool for a specific technique — **RNA End-Joining**
(REJ) — and most of the parameters in the app only make sense once you
know what that technique is trying to do. This page gives you the
background. If you already know all this, skip to
[How it works](/docs/how-it-works).

## The technique in one paragraph

RNA End-Joining is a method for expressing a protein from **two separate
RNA fragments** that get joined together in the cell, rather than from a
single full-length transcript. You design a coding sequence, split it
into a 5′ half and a 3′ half at a specific kind of motif, deliver each
half as its own transcript, and rely on the cell's end-joining
machinery to fuse them back into the original protein. The protein is
the same; the delivery is split.

## Why split a coding sequence at all?

A few reasons people care about this:

- **Payload size.** Some delivery vehicles (AAV, LNP, etc.) cap the
  amount of RNA you can carry per particle. Splitting a large CDS
  across two particles gets you past that ceiling without redesigning
  the protein.
- **Modularity.** Each half can be optimized, expressed, or replaced
  independently — useful during development, and especially useful
  when you want to swap one half for a different variant.
- **Safety.** A split design can be "armed" only when both halves are
  present, which is handy for constructs where uncontrolled expression
  is a problem.

None of these concerns matter if the protein still comes out wrong. The
whole point of sequence design for REJ is to make sure the split happens
at a place the cell machinery will _actually_ rejoin, and that each
half is well-behaved as its own transcript.

## What makes REJ-ready sequence different from a normal CDS?

Two big things:

1. **The join has to land on a WGGW motif.** `WGGW` (where `W` is A or T)
   is the recognition substrate for the end-joining reaction — your
   split point needs one, ideally one close to the middle of the CDS so
   the two halves are balanced. See
   [the glossary](/docs/glossary#wggw-motif) for the full definition.
2. **Each fragment has to behave like a proper transcript on its own.**
   That means no cryptic splice sites silently deleting exons, no
   repeat-heavy regions that make synthesis flaky, controlled GC
   content, and (optionally) stimulatory introns to help expression.
   These are the knobs in the [design tool](/docs/design-tool).

Everything REJ Studio does is in service of those two requirements:
find a good split point, then clean up each half so it expresses
reliably.

## Where REJ Studio fits

REJ Studio doesn't run the end-joining experiment. It's the **design
stage** — the part where you pick a gene, inspect its transcripts, let
the splitter find a balanced WGGW cut, run the optimizer over each
fragment, and download the result. You take the downloaded sequences
into your synthesis + delivery pipeline from there.

Think of it as a constraint-aware codon optimizer that understands the
REJ-specific requirements (WGGW join, fragment balance, splice
hygiene), not a general-purpose molecular biology platform.

## What REJ Studio does _not_ do

- **No wet-lab validation.** The optimizer is deterministic; biology is
  not. Every sequence you design here should be tested before you
  trust it.
- **No claim about the specific end-joining chemistry.** The tool is
  agnostic to which end-joining pathway you're relying on — it just
  makes sure the sequence meets the structural requirements.
- **No prediction of expression, stability, or immunogenicity.** Those
  are downstream questions REJ Studio doesn't try to answer.
