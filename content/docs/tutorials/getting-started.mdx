---
title: Design your first REJ sequence
description: A hands-on walkthrough from gene search to optimized download, using TP53 as a worked example.
---

This tutorial walks you through a complete REJ Studio session — from
searching for a gene to downloading an optimized sequence. By the end
you'll have designed, optimized, and exported a split TP53 construct
ready for synthesis.

No biology background is required to follow along, but if you want
context on _why_ you're doing each step, see
[What is RNA End-Joining?](/docs/explanation/rna-end-joining).

## What you'll need

- A browser (any modern browser works).
- About five minutes.

## 1. Search for a gene

Open REJ Studio and type `TP53` into the search box. The first result
should be **TP53 — tumor protein p53** (human). Click it.

<Callout type="info">
You can also search by full gene name, Ensembl ID, MIM number, or
disease term. See [Search syntax](/docs/reference/search-syntax) for
the full list.
</Callout>

## 2. Pick an isoform

You'll land on the gene detail page showing every annotated transcript.
Each row lists the transcript ID, biotype, length, and exon count.

For this tutorial, pick the **longest protein-coding isoform** — it's
at the top of the list. Click **Open in design tool** on that row.

## 3. Review the pre-filled form

The design tool opens with the CDS already loaded. You'll see:

- **Species** set to _Homo sapiens_ (inherited from the gene).
- **Splice junction** defaulting to the WGGW motif closest to the
  midpoint of the CDS — this gives you a balanced split.
- **Optimization parameters** at their defaults (all enabled).

For your first run, leave everything as-is. The defaults are designed
to produce a well-optimized sequence without any tuning.

## 4. Submit the job

Click **Submit**. The form posts to the optimization backend, which
splits the CDS at the selected WGGW motif and runs each fragment
through the constraint solver independently.

This usually takes a few seconds.

## 5. Read the results

When the job finishes you'll see:

- **Two optimized fragments** (5' and 3') — same protein, rewritten
  DNA. The split bar shows how balanced they are:

<SplitBarShowcase />

- **Diagnostic badges** in the header — a quick-read summary of the
  optimization:

<DiagBadgeShowcase />

- **An optimization report** — a per-constraint breakdown showing
  which objectives were satisfied and what changed.

Check that everything is green. If a constraint shows as "violated",
don't worry — see [Troubleshooting](/docs/guides/troubleshooting) for
common fixes.

## 6. Download

Click **Download FASTA** to save the optimized fragments. The filename
includes the gene symbol so you can stack multiple designs without
collisions.

<Callout type="warn">
Results live in the browser session only. If you close the tab, the
job is gone. Always download before navigating away.
</Callout>

## What to try next

Now that you've completed a default run, try these variations:

- **Change the split point.** Drag the splice junction slider to a
  different WGGW motif and compare the report.
- **Toggle an objective off.** Disable CpG minimization, re-run, and
  see how the result changes.
- **Bump a weight.** Increase the codon optimization weight to 2x and
  compare codon usage scores.
- **Try a different gene.** Search for `BRCA1` or `ATM` — longer CDSs
  give the optimizer more room to work with.
