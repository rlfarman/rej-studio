---
title: Using the design tool
description: What each form field does, and how to choose sensible defaults.
---

The design tool takes a coding sequence (CDS), a split point, and a set
of optimization parameters, then runs each fragment through the
optimizer. You can open it two ways:

1. **From an isoform** — the CDS is pre-filled and the gene symbol is
   attached for reference.
2. **Standalone** — paste your own CDS. It must start with `ATG`, end
   with a stop codon, and be a multiple of three.

## Input

### Species

Sets the codon usage table the optimizer adapts to. Pick the organism
you'll express the sequence in — human and mouse have different usage
preferences and the wrong choice will push codon optimization in the
wrong direction.

### Splice junction

The point where REJ Studio splits your CDS into 5′ and 3′ fragments.
The slider defaults to the [WGGW motif](/docs/glossary#wggw-motif)
closest to the midpoint — a balanced split with a valid RNA End-Joining
substrate at the joint. Drag it to pick a different motif if you have a
reason to.

## Optimization

### Remove cryptic splice sites [#remove-cryptic-splice-sites]

Scans each fragment for cryptic 5′ and 3′ splice site motifs and
rewrites them to break the motif without changing the protein.
Recommended for any design intended to end up in a transcribed context
— unintended splicing can silently delete internal exons.

### Minimize CpG sites [#minimize-cpgs]

Reduces the number of [CpG dinucleotides](/docs/glossary#cpg-dinucleotide).
CpGs are substrates for methylation and can trigger innate immune
sensing. Lowering them is standard practice for therapeutic RNA and
synthetic constructs.

### Reduce k-mer complexity [#kmer-complexity]

Penalizes repeated 10-mers across the sequence.
[High-complexity sequences](/docs/glossary#k-mer-complexity) synthesize
more reliably and behave better during manufacturing and sequencing.

### Enforce 35–60% GC content [#gc-content]

A **hard constraint** keeping GC content inside the mammalian-friendly
range over a sliding window. If the solver can't hit the window given
your other constraints, you'll see it called out in the report. Leave
this on unless you have a specific reason to run without a GC
constraint.

## Stimulatory introns

Optional. REJ Studio can insert a short optimized intron into either
fragment to boost expression. The protein is unchanged but each intron
adds length.

### 5′ stimulatory intron [#stim-5]

Inserts an intron into the 5′ fragment. Useful when the 5′ half is the
limiting piece for expression.

### 3′ stimulatory intron [#stim-3]

Inserts an intron into the 3′ fragment. Useful when the 3′ half is the
limiting piece.

Enable one, both, or neither based on downstream context. They stack
cleanly with the rest of the optimization.

## Parameter weights

Each soft objective has a weight that controls how much it pulls on the
final sequence relative to the others. You only need to touch these
when the defaults give you a result that's good at one thing and weak
at another — bump the weight of whatever you care more about.

- **Codon optimization weight** — how hard the solver pushes toward the
  species-preferred codon table.
- **Cryptic splice site weight** — how hard it pushes to eliminate
  splice-like motifs.
- **CpG minimization weight** — how hard it pushes CpG count down.
- **K-mer complexity weight** — how hard it pushes to break up repeated
  k-mers.

Hard constraints (GC content) always take priority over any weight.

## Tips

- **Start with defaults, then tighten.** Run once with defaults, read
  the [report](/docs/results), and bump only the weights that matter
  for your specific use.
- **Watch for objective conflicts.** Maximum codon optimization and
  aggressive motif removal sometimes pull in opposite directions. The
  report will tell you which objective is hardest to satisfy.
- **Don't over-tune weights.** Big weight swings on one objective
  usually come at the cost of everything else. 2× is a big move.
