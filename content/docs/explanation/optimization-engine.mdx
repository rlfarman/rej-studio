## The pipeline

When you submit a job, REJ Studio runs through these stages:

1. **Optimize.** The full CDS is passed to DNAChisel with your chosen
   constraints and objectives. The solver searches for a synonymous
   rewrite that satisfies hard constraints and minimizes the weighted
   sum of soft objectives. Translation is enforced — every codon swap
   is synonymous.
2. **Anchor the split.** The optimized CDS is scanned for a
   WGGW-compatible dipeptide near your chosen split position; if one
   is found within the distance threshold, the codons there are
   rewritten (translation still preserved) to install a WGGW motif at
   the junction. If stimulatory introns are enabled, the same
   procedure places a `[STIMINTRON]` placeholder ~150 bp upstream or
   downstream of the split.
3. **Split and return.** The sequence is cleaved at the junction into
   5′ and 3′ fragments and a per-objective report is returned to the
   browser.

The backend is a Python/FastAPI service running DNAChisel. In
development it runs locally; in production it runs on Modal.

## DNAChisel: constraint-based codon optimization

[DNAChisel](https://edinburgh-genome-foundry.github.io/DnaChisel/) is
an open-source library that treats sequence optimization as a
**constraint-satisfaction problem**. It doesn't "invent" sequences —
it searches for a coding-equivalent sequence (same amino acids,
different DNA) that satisfies the constraints you define.

Think of it as rewriting the DNA without changing the protein, while
steering away from patterns you don't want and toward patterns you do.

## Hard constraints vs. soft objectives

REJ Studio exposes two kinds of rules:

| Type | Behavior | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard constraint** | Must be satisfied. If the solver can't meet it, the constraint is reported as violated. | GC content (35-60%) |
| **Soft objective** | Should be minimized, weighted against other objectives. | CpG minimization, cryptic splice removal, k-mer complexity, codon adaptation |

Hard constraints always take priority. When the solver can't satisfy a
hard constraint given the soft objectives, it satisfies the hard
constraint and reports which soft objectives it had to compromise on.

## How weights work

Each soft objective has a numerical weight. The solver tries to
minimize a weighted sum of all objective scores simultaneously. A
higher weight means that objective pulls harder on the final sequence.

When two objectives conflict (e.g. codon optimization wants one codon,
CpG minimization wants a different one), weights determine who wins.
The report shows the per-objective scores so you can see where
compromises landed.

## What the optimizer can and cannot do

**Can:**
- Swap synonymous codons (same protein, different DNA).
- Penalize and generally avoid sequence motifs (cryptic splice
  donors/acceptors, repeated 10-mers, CpG dinucleotides).
- Enforce global properties over sliding windows (35–60% GC content).
- Mark positions where stimulatory introns can be placed during
  downstream synthesis.

**Cannot:**
- Change the encoded protein. Every codon swap is synonymous.
- Insert an actual intron sequence — it emits a `[STIMINTRON]`
  placeholder that you substitute at synthesis time.
- Predict translation efficiency, mRNA stability, protein folding,
  immunogenicity, or REJ rejoining efficiency.
- Guarantee that the biological outcome matches the sequence design —
  that requires experimental validation.
- Store or remember previous runs. Each submission is independent.

## Why results can vary slightly

DNAChisel's solver has some stochastic elements. When multiple
coding-equivalent sequences score equally well, the solver may return
different ones across runs. If you need exact reproducibility, save
the FASTA output — don't rely on re-running to reproduce a result.
