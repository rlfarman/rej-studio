## Open the design tool

You can get there two ways:

1. **From an isoform** — the CDS is pre-filled and the gene symbol is
   attached.
2. **Standalone** — paste your own CDS. It must start with `ATG`, end
   with a stop codon, and be a multiple of three.

## Pick a split point

The split-point slider defaults to the WGGW motif closest to the CDS
midpoint — a balanced split with a WGGW substrate at the joint. Drag
it to pick a different motif if you have a reason to (e.g. avoiding a
problematic region).

## Choose your optimization parameters

Start with the defaults. They enable all objectives at balanced
weights — good for a first pass. See
[Parameters reference](/docs/reference/parameters) for what each one
does in detail.

If you want to customize:

- **Toggle objectives on/off** depending on what matters for your
  construct. Not every design needs CpG minimization, for example.
- **Adjust weights** only when the defaults give you a result that's
  strong at one thing and weak at another. Bump the weight of whatever
  you care more about. 2x is a big move — don't over-swing.
- **Enable stimulatory introns** (5', 3', or both) if expression is
  limiting. The protein is unchanged but each intron adds length.

## Submit and wait

Click **Submit**. The backend splits at the selected WGGW motif and
optimizes each fragment independently. This usually takes a few
seconds.

## Iterate

The form remembers your last parameters. The most productive workflow
is:

1. **Run once with defaults**, read the
   [report](/docs/guides/reading-results).
2. **Change one thing at a time** — toggle an objective, bump a weight,
   move the split point.
3. **Compare reports** to understand which constraint was doing what.

### Tips

- **Watch for objective conflicts.** Maximum codon optimization and
  aggressive motif removal sometimes pull in opposite directions. The
  report will show which objective is hardest to satisfy.
- **Don't over-tune weights.** Big swings on one objective usually
  cost everything else.
- **When in doubt, widen a constraint.** Loosening is usually right
  when the solver is trapped; tightening is for when the result is
  too permissive.
