---
title: Reading your results
description: How to interpret the optimization output and decide what to do next.
---

When a job finishes you'll see three things: the **optimized
sequence**, an **optimization report**, and a set of **diagnostic
badges**.

## Check the diagnostic badges

The header shows quick-read indicators:

- **GC%** — overall GC content. Green if inside your target range.
- **CpG count** — number of CpG dinucleotides. Lower is usually
  better.
- **Max k-mer repeat** — the most-repeated k-mer. Helpful for
  spotting complexity issues at a glance.
- **Length** — total bp, for sanity-checking against your input.

These badges are a summary, not a substitute for the full report.

## Read the optimization report

The report gives a per-constraint breakdown of what the solver did.
For each constraint you enabled you'll see:

- Whether it was **satisfied**, **partially satisfied**, or
  **violated**.
- The **score** the solver ended on (lower is better for most
  constraints).
- A short **explanation** of what changed — e.g. "3 CpG sites
  removed", "splice site at position 412 neutralized".

A constraint that shows "violated" is usually either impossible given
your other constraints, or impossible given the amino acid sequence.
The report will tell you which.

## Handle violations

If a constraint is violated:

1. **Relax or disable the offending constraint** and re-run. Start
   with the lowest-priority objective.
2. **Check for conflicts** — aggressive codon optimization and CpG
   minimization can fight the GC window.
3. **Check your input** — if it's already outside 35-60% GC, the
   solver may not be able to recover.

See [Troubleshooting](/docs/guides/troubleshooting) for specific
error messages.

## Download and save

Click **Download FASTA** to save the optimized fragments. The
filename includes the gene symbol (or `Custom` if you pasted your
own).

<Callout type="warn">
Results live in the browser session only. Download before closing
the tab — there is no way to recover a lost result.
</Callout>

## Validate experimentally

The optimizer is deterministic given the same input, but biology
isn't. Nothing in the report predicts expression, stability, or
function — that still has to be measured. Every sequence designed here
should be tested before you trust it.
